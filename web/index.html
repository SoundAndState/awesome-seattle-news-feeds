About this reader

Sound & State

Why this reader exists

Sound & State was made for testing changes to the Awesome Seattle News Feeds list. It is not a commercial service. The maintainer, (sayhiben), runs it without a profit motive.

Privacy Statement

  • The reader does not set or read cookies, use analytics, or load fonts from other sites.
  • The reader keeps saved items, which items you have read, and preferences in this browser. It does not upload or sync them.
  • Clearing this site's browser data deletes your library and preferences. If browser storage is unavailable, you lose changes when you close or reload the page.
  • GitHub hosts this site; Cloudflare delivers feeds. Both receive your IP address and the page or feed you request. Both may retain service and security records.
  • If Cloudflare cannot deliver a feed, your browser may request it directly from the publisher. The publisher then receives your IP address and feed request.
  • Feed requests send no cookies, login credentials, or address of the page you are viewing.
  • Publisher and archive links open in a new tab. Those sites apply their own privacy policies and may use cookies.
How the reader connects and displays content

The maintainer disables stored request logs and tracing for the feed service. The service uses your IP address to limit repeated requests. Cloudflare and GitHub may still keep service and security records under their own policies. These settings do not guarantee that those companies retain no information about your visits.

Your browser loads feeds through Cloudflare. If Cloudflare cannot get a feed from the publisher, it tries a previously saved copy, when one is available. If Cloudflare still cannot provide the feed, your browser tries to load it directly from the publisher.

For that direct attempt to work, the publisher must allow other websites, including this reader, to read its feed in a browser. This permission is called cross-origin resource sharing (CORS). Without it, your browser blocks the reader from accessing the feed.

When this reader loads a feed, your browser does not save any cookies the publisher sends. The reader keeps track of which items you have read in this browser and never sends that information to a server. When you search or change views, the reader puts your selection after the # in the page address. Your browser does not send that part of the address to GitHub.

The reader removes scripts, embedded pages, forms, and images from feed previews before showing them. Your browser also limits which scripts the reader can run and which sites it can contact.

When you follow a publisher or archive link, your browser does not send the address of this reader to that site. Choose Find archived page to send the article address to Ghostarchive and look for a saved copy. The reader first removes tracking parameters whose names start with utm_. Ghostarchive may not have a copy.

How the reader refreshes and stores items

While you view Articles or Posts, the reader checks the feeds that match your filters. It pauses those checks when you leave the tab and checks again when you return. After a feed loads successfully, the reader waits at least 15 minutes before checking it again. If a feed fails, the reader waits longer between attempts. Choose Refresh to retry failed feeds immediately.

When the reader finds new items while you are reading, it shows a button above the list. Choose that button to add the new items when you are ready.

The reader checks your browser’s stored feeds before showing a progress bar. If feeds need checking, wait for “Ready to read” before you start reading. The bar counts completed checks, including feeds the reader could not load. It does not estimate how much time remains. The finished status stays in place until you choose Dismiss feed status or change views.

The reader keeps unsaved items for up to 30 days and up to 150 items per feed. It keeps saved items until you remove them or clear this site's browser data.

Cloudflare keeps copies of feeds for up to 15 minutes so it can reuse them across requests. It keeps them for less time when the publisher requires it, and it does not store error responses or content the publisher marks as private.

GitHub also runs a scheduled task to collect backup copies of the KING 5, KREM 2, and MyNorthwest feeds. The task runs every 15 minutes, though GitHub may delay it. Cloudflare stops using each backup within 6 hours, or sooner if the publisher requires it. Browse publications to see when the task collected a copy. The task does not send your information to publishers.

The reader keeps a copy of the feed list in your browser so you can still browse your library if it cannot download the latest list. You need an internet connection to load new content, and the reader may not reopen while you are offline.

You can turn on Mark items read as I scroll past them in Reading options. Once you enable it, the reader marks an item as read when you scroll it past the top of the screen.

Back up your library

Choose Export reading backup to download a JSON file with your saved articles and posts and a record of which items you have read. Choose Restore backup to add items from a backup and combine its read and saved marks with your current library. You can also choose Export saved items as CSV above the reading list to download all your saved items for a spreadsheet.
