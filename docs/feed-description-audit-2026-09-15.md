## Assessment

The catalog generally describes publishers respectfully. I found no explicit racial slurs, claims of group inferiority, or statements that community journalism is less legitimate in the 119 feed descriptions. Several descriptions can nevertheless represent their sources more fairly and accurately. The most important changes concern omitted communities and partners, a publisher’s relationship to the people it serves, and inconsistent treatment of reporting, opinion, advocacy, and institutional interests.

This is an assessment of wording and foreseeable framing effects. It cannot establish anyone’s subconscious beliefs or intent, demonstrate discriminatory impact on readers, or certify the catalog as free of bias. A source’s self-description is evidence of how it identifies its work, not independent proof of every claim it makes about accuracy, independence, or public value.

All 119 entries are inventoried below: 92 publication, organization, and agency feeds and 27 Bluesky accounts. Every entry includes the exact catalog description, a publisher-controlled source, an individual assessment, and a recommendation. Categories receive a separate review because placement can shape interpretation as much as wording. The original review proposed wording separately from the catalog; the implementation status above records the accepted changes.

## Findings that deserve attention first

### Preserve community identity and community agency

- **Northwest Asian Weekly:** its About page explicitly includes the AAPI community. The catalog names Asian Americans but omits Pacific Islanders. The phrase “plus broader community issues” can also make a community publisher’s coverage sound like a narrow identity topic with ordinary civic news added on. Describe a publication serving Asian American and Pacific Islander communities instead. This is the clearest correction to a named community in the news descriptions. [Publisher About page](https://nwasianweekly.com/about/)
- **The Seattle Medium:** “Black communities” is respectful language. The issue is that “reporting on” makes those communities the object of coverage while the publisher describes a newspaper serving them. Its institutional description uses African American and names a wider service area. Follow that self-description without implying that Black and African American are interchangeable terms for every person or publisher. [Publisher About page](https://seattlemedium.com/tiloben-publishing-co-inc/)
- **Converge Media:** the existing emphasis on Black communities accurately reflects its mission. There is no fairness benefit in erasing that emphasis. Its own description gives a useful refinement: Pacific Northwest storytelling for Black and urban audiences. Here, urban is the publisher’s term, not a racial euphemism supplied by the catalog. [Publisher About page](https://www.whereweconverge.com/about)
- **South Seattle Emerald:** the current description is respectful. Adding community-led would better express the newsroom’s own account of who shapes its journalism. Its racial-justice lens is not sufficient reason to reclassify its reporting as advocacy, nor does South Seattle’s location justify assigning the newsroom or its readers a single racial identity. [Publisher mission](https://southseattleemerald.org/about)
- **Washington Smoke Blog:** the publisher names state, county, and federal agencies **and Tribes** as partners. The catalog’s generic reference to public agencies leaves that explicit attribution out. Name the partners as the publisher does; do not recast Tribes simply as cultural groups. [Publisher introduction](https://wasmoke.blogspot.com)

The three descriptions that explicitly name racial or cultural communities—The Seattle Medium, Northwest Asian Weekly, and Converge—are all in **Seattle and Regional Reporting**, alongside other newsrooms. That is a sound choice. They are not relegated to a separate ethnic-news category or placed under culture merely because of whom they serve. “Community” is also used for outlets across the region, including Wallyhood, My Edmonds News, and Salish Current; the word itself is not being reserved for publishers serving racialized communities. These observations come from the catalog snapshot, not assumptions about publishers’ ownership or readers’ identities.

### Use the same standards for genre and institutional interests

**Opinion is not disclosed consistently.** The Bulletin describes news and views and identifies progressive parent founders. HardPressed explicitly includes editorial work. The STAND explicitly includes opinion. Lynnwood Times’ current feed contains publisher-labeled opinion alongside reporting. Naming those forms would bring the descriptions closer to the standard already used for The Stranger, MyNorthwest, and Seattle Schools Community Forum. It does not require inventing an ideology for an entire newsroom. [The Bulletin](https://thebulletinsea.org/about/), [HardPressed](https://www.hardpressed-info.com/about/), [The STAND](https://www.thestand.org/about/), [Lynnwood Times feed](https://lynnwoodtimes.com/feed/)

**Some labels that might initially look imposed are supported.** The Urbanist calls its work advocacy journalism; Ryan Packer identifies as a transportation advocate; Through the Static expressly identifies as antifascist; Naomi Ishisaka names Social Justice Columnist as a professional role. Cascadia Journal itself uses opinionated. These words should not be treated as bias findings simply because they describe values or a political subject. In Cascadia Journal’s case, the proposed change to commentary is only a consistency refinement. [The Urbanist](https://www.theurbanist.org/about-us/), [Ryan Packer](https://bsky.app/profile/typewriteralley.bsky.social), [Through the Static](https://throughthestatic.org), [Naomi Ishisaka](https://bsky.app/profile/naomiishisaka.bsky.social), [Cascadia Journal](https://www.cascadia-journal.com/about/)

**Qualifiers can be accurate yet uneven.** Puget Sound Anarchists explicitly accepts signed and anonymous submissions; naming only the latter presents an incomplete account. “Mixed with” can suggest that essays or features dilute reporting; use parallel descriptions for both The Harbor Rat Report and The Seattle Times Real Estate. “Preferred” in the Times Local Politics entry is the catalog curator’s unexplained preference, not a publisher characteristic. The remedy is precision and consistency, not stripping out useful disclosures. [Puget Sound Anarchists](https://pugetsoundanarchists.org/about/), [Sabot Media](https://sabot.media/about/), [Seattle Times Real Estate](https://www.seattletimes.com/business/real-estate)

**Commercial and official interests deserve the same clarity as activist interests.** Urban Living already identifies the brokerage behind its information, and SouthSoundTalk already discloses sponsored stories. Visit Seattle could more explicitly name its tourism-marketing function, and the Junction Association could more clearly name its business-association role. Keep the distinction between police incident reports and independently established facts. An official source is not automatically neutral; an advocacy source is not automatically unreliable. [Urban Living](https://www.urbnlivn.com/page/about), [SouthSoundTalk](https://www.southsoundtalk.com/about/), [Visit Seattle](https://visitseattle.org/about-us/), [Junction Association](https://wsjunction.org/about/), [SPD Blotter](https://spdblotter.seattle.gov)

**The same publisher should retain the same identity across channels.** The Urbanist’s site entry uses policy-position shorthand while its Bluesky entry uses advocacy journalism. Through the Static’s site entry omits the outlook its Bluesky entry names. The Stranger’s progressive commentary appears in one description but not the other. Channel scope can differ; the publisher’s identity should not vary accidentally. The corresponding entries below identify which language is publisher-supported.

### Keep the conclusion proportionate to the evidence

There is a plausible cumulative framing risk when some sources receive qualifiers about advocacy, anonymity, personal campaigns, or irregular publication while comparable sources receive only reporting and analysis. This review identifies concrete examples that merit correction; it does **not** establish a racial pattern in those choices. Differences can also reflect genuinely different genres, institutional roles, and feed contents.

The topic lists for Burien, SeaTac, White Center, and other South King County sources generally include ordinary civic, business, and community life. Tukwila’s public-safety reference merits the same scope discipline applied elsewhere, but the publisher itself includes crime coverage. A single supported topic is not proof that the catalog stereotypes an entire neighborhood.

No publisher’s name, neighborhood, staff photograph, surname, or choice of reporting topic was used to infer race, ethnicity, religion, sexual orientation, or Indigenous ownership. In particular, Salish Current’s name and land acknowledgment do not establish that it is a Tribal publication. [Salish Current’s account of itself](https://salish-current.org/about/)

## Editorial standard to use going forward

1. **Start with the publisher’s own About page, mission, section description, or account bio.** Use its preferred community names and professional roles. Keep a source URL and review date in editorial notes so future changes can be checked.
2. **Write a concise, attributed paraphrase.** Borrow meaningful identity and genre terms; do not copy whole promotional paragraphs. Omit unverified superlatives such as premier, most trusted, or leading. Self-description is a starting point, not a substitute for checking observable behavior.
3. **Separate audience, authorship, ownership, geography, genre, and viewpoint.** Reporting about a community does not establish that a publisher is owned by, led by, or speaks for that community. A person’s disclosed identity need not appear in a professional feed summary unless it is relevant to that summary.
4. **Recognize the people doing the work.** When supported, describe publishers serving communities, community-led newsrooms, member-run unions, and worker-owned publications. Avoid turning people into only the subjects of poverty, crime, discrimination, or institutional intervention.
5. **Apply genre labels consistently and specifically.** Reporting, analysis, opinion, advocacy, official announcements, advertising, and satire can coexist. Describe the actual mix and relevant relationships; do not infer unreliability from a mission or neutrality from an established brand. Independent ownership or funding is not the same as political neutrality.
6. **Keep useful limits, but put them in the right place.** Paywalls, feed overlap, irregular publication, and section selection can help readers. Apply one policy to comparable feeds, date observations that can change, and avoid turning temporary conditions into permanent judgments about a publisher.
7. **Check duplicate publishers together.** A website feed and its Bluesky account can carry different material, but self-identification and professional titles should remain consistent unless there is evidence of a real difference.
8. **Use a comparison question in review:** would the same wording seem warranted for an established daily, a business association, a public agency, and a community-led publisher doing similar work? Different answers can be justified, but the reason should be the source’s role and content rather than assumptions about its community.

## Scope, evidence, and limits

- The inventory covers every feed ID and every category in the current [catalog](../data/feeds.json). It also accounts for the four archive links, which have names but no descriptions. Existing [contribution criteria](../.github/CONTRIBUTING.md) require accurate genre labels; the proposed standard gives that requirement a consistent application.
- Publisher evidence was retrieved from public websites, About pages, section metadata, and all 27 public Bluesky profile records. Profile records were read through Bluesky’s public service using each catalog account’s stable identifier; the report links to the corresponding human-readable profile. Evidence summaries below are paraphrases unless quotation marks identify an exact excerpt.
- Targeted feed samples were read for 17 entries where genre, overlap, or cadence affected the assessment. Sixteen were retrieved; Real Change returned 403. These samples help establish content types but are not a quantitative content analysis, a full archive review, or a feed-health certification. Other entries rely on their publisher pages and profiles, as identified individually.
- **Real Change was the one entry with an unresolved current-source limitation in the original audit.** Its site and feed blocked retrieval. The original assessment used an indexed excerpt of its own 2025 media kit and marked the proposed addition provisional. The implementation note above records the additional publisher-controlled evidence that resolved this gap. It does not substitute a third party’s opinion or an unverified mirror for the publisher’s account.
- This audit evaluates descriptions and category framing, not the truth of publishers’ individual stories, the conduct of their staff, or the representativeness of the catalog’s selection. Determining whether the selection omits important cultural or language communities would require a separate coverage inventory; this review does not claim to have established that.
- Proposed sentences summarize the checked evidence. They are editorial recommendations, not publisher-approved quotations or automatic replacements. The original audit left feed IDs, addresses, categories, verification dates, and generated files unchanged. See the implementation status for the subsequent catalog edits.

## Recommendation key

- **Keep:** no material fairness or source-fidelity correction identified in the description. This is not a claim that the publisher or all its coverage is unbiased.
- **Revise:** a specific source mismatch, omission, or inconsistent framing warrants a change. This category includes factual and editorial issues; it does not mean discriminatory intent was found.
- **Refine:** the wording is defensible, with an optional improvement in precision, self-description, or consistency.
- **Verify:** the assessment identifies a plausible improvement, but current publisher evidence is incomplete.

### Satire (1 feeds)

**Current:** Local humor and satirical news.

**Assessment:** The label identifies an actual genre and helps readers interpret fictional stories. It is not a credibility warning attached to a cultural group.

### Bluesky (27 feeds)

**Current:** Social feeds from Seattle-region journalists, publications, and commentators.

**Assessment:** This is a delivery-platform category containing reporters, commentators, publications, advocates, and satire. Individual descriptions must carry those distinctions. Align the identity of duplicate publishers across channels and do not imply that personal accounts are employer-controlled newswires.
